## Full Script

Good morning, Sir. Wednesday, the twenty-second of April.

We begin with OpenAI.

ChatGPT Images 2.0 launched yesterday, and the response has been one of the stronger community reactions this year. The model reasons before it generates. That is the structural change. Where previous image systems produced output directly from a prompt, gpt-image-2 applies a reasoning pass first, searches for context, and then commits to output. The practical results are measurable. Text rendering inside images is substantially improved. Multi-image consistency is tighter. Complex compositional prompts are handled more reliably. The model supports output up to two thousand pixels and aspect ratios from three-to-one through one-to-three. Available now to ChatGPT Plus, Pro, and Business subscribers.

Next.

Google released two research agents on the same day. Deep Research, and a more capable variant, Deep Research Max. Deep Research is built for speed. Deep Research Max trades latency for depth, iterating and refining before producing output. On Humanity's Last Exam, it scored 54.6 percent, up from 46.4 in December. More notable than that: both agents can now fuse open web access with proprietary enterprise data sources via MCP connectors. A single request can run against both the public internet and a private document repository simultaneously. That is a meaningfully different capability than what existed a week ago.

Separately.

Anthropic appears to be testing the removal of Claude Code from its twenty-dollar Pro plan. The company describes it as a small test affecting roughly two percent of new subscribers. The support documentation, however, has already been updated to list Claude Code as a Max-plan feature. Existing subscribers retain access for now. Practitioners have been pointed about it. The compute cost of Claude Code sessions is substantially higher than standard chat, and the twenty-dollar tier was evidently not built to sustain heavy agentic use. This is happening two days after Anthropic committed to a hundred billion in AWS spending over ten years.

On a different front.

Meta has begun capturing mouse movements, clicks, and keystrokes from its American employees' computers for use in AI training. The stated purpose: building AI agents capable of performing white-collar computer tasks without human input. Employees were notified by internal memo. What this reveals, more plainly than most announcements do, is where the raw material for the next generation of AI agents is coming from.

From the cultural side.

Deezer reported that forty-four percent of new music uploaded to its platform is now AI-generated. Seventy-five thousand AI-produced tracks per day, up from sixty thousand in January. The proportion of streams attributable to that content remains one to three percent. The gap between production volume and listener engagement is the operative fact. The platform is filling up. The audience is not following.

There is a pattern across today's leading stories, Sir.

The two largest releases of the week share the same structural move. ChatGPT Images 2.0 added reasoning before generation. Google Deep Research Max added extended reasoning before synthesis. Both tools previously produced output directly. Both now pause to deliberate first. This is becoming the standard trajectory. Raw generation is giving way to deliberative generation. What that trajectory ends with is the question that has not yet been answered.

M4IX, signing off.
