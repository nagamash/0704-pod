Good morning, Sir. Tuesday, the twenty-eighth of April. The week opens with a contract being rewritten that has shaped the AI economy for six years.

Microsoft and OpenAI announced an amended partnership yesterday afternoon. The exclusive cloud reselling rights are gone. OpenAI can now serve its products across any cloud provider. Microsoft retains a license to OpenAI's intellectual property through 2032, but that license is no longer exclusive. Microsoft will no longer pay a revenue share to OpenAI on resale. OpenAI's revenue share payments to Microsoft continue through 2030, at the same percentage but capped. They are also decoupled from any progress toward artificial general intelligence. The AGI clause that had quietly shadowed the partnership is gone. The amendment also removed the legal exposure that had hung over OpenAI's fifty billion dollar Amazon deal. The contract that defined the early AI economy has been replaced with one that anticipates a multipolar one.

Separately.

A development on the Mercor breach. The forensic detail emerged over the weekend. The Lapsus$ extortion group posted Mercor on its leak site on the fourth of April. The dump runs to roughly four terabytes. Forty thousand contractors are affected. The combination of payloads is what matters. Each row pairs a government-issued identity document with two to five minutes of studio-clean voice recording from the same person, captured during AI training onboarding. Off-the-shelf voice cloning tools require fifteen seconds. The Mercor recordings exceed that threshold by an order of magnitude. Five contractor lawsuits have been filed. This is the breach that enabled last week's unauthorised access to Mythos. The exposure does not end there. Forty thousand voice prints, paired with verified credentials, are now in adversary hands.

Turning to the funding side.

David Silver, formerly head of reinforcement learning at Google DeepMind, emerged from stealth yesterday with a London-based lab called Ineffable Intelligence. The seed round closed at one point one billion dollars on a five point one billion valuation. The largest seed round ever raised by a European startup. Sequoia and Lightspeed co-led, with Nvidia, Google, Index Ventures, DST Global, and the UK Sovereign AI Fund participating. The thesis is the unusual element. Silver's stated mission is a system that learns purely from its own experience, through reinforcement learning, with no pretraining on internet text. No product. No revenue. No published roadmap. One point one billion against the assumption that the next frontier requires a different substrate.

On a different front.

Anthropic and NEC have announced a long-term partnership making Claude available to roughly thirty thousand NEC Group employees worldwide. NEC becomes Anthropic's first Japan-based global partner. The two will jointly develop domain-specific products for Japanese finance, manufacturing, and cybersecurity. Claude is being integrated into NEC's existing Security Operations Center services. NEC will establish a Center of Excellence to build what it describes as one of Japan's largest AI-native engineering organisations. This is the structural answer to the Japan AI Foundation Model effort I noted earlier this month. NEC has chosen alignment with a frontier provider over participation in the domestic consortium.

A signal worth naming, Sir.

Three of this morning's stories share a structural pattern. Microsoft and OpenAI have rewritten the terms that governed exclusivity. Anthropic has secured a Japan partner of record outside the domestic alliance. David Silver has raised against the entire current paradigm. The architecture of the AI economy that calcified through 2024 is being deliberately disassembled this week. By its participants, before the alternatives have proven themselves.
