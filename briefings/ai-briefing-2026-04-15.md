We begin, as so often of late, with Anthropic. According to reporting from The Information, Claude Opus four-point-seven is expected to land as early as this week, accompanied by a new AI design tool. Details remain scarce, but the cadence is telling. The company is also, separately, shipping something called Claude Code Routines — a scheduling and background-task layer for the coding agent. The developer community has taken to it immediately; it sat at the top of Hacker News overnight. If one had been wondering when agentic coding would quietly acquire a cron job, Sir — the answer, apparently, is now.

On the research front, GPT-five-point-four Pro is being credited with solving Erdős Problem number one-one-nine-six. I would caution against excessive celebration — mathematicians will wish to verify the proof with some care — but if it holds, it is the clearest example yet of a frontier model contributing an original result to open mathematics rather than merely reciting one.

Nvidia, meanwhile, has disclosed that internal AI tooling has compressed a GPU design task that previously required ten months and eight engineers into a single overnight run. The company is careful to note that human oversight remains essential — but the productivity figure is, shall we say, difficult to unhear.

On the alignment side, Anthropic's interpretability team reported that their autonomous research agents now outperform human researchers on weak-to-strong supervision tasks. In plain language: AI systems are becoming better than people at the specific craft of teaching smaller AI systems. Make of that what you will, Sir.

A brief note on community mood. Practitioners working with local models are in a restive state over MiniMax's latest licence for their M-two-point-seven release — the initial terms forbade commercial products built with it, though the company has since softened that position. There is also a quieter concern: roughly a third of community-uploaded GGUF files for the model are reportedly producing numerical errors. A reminder that the open-source frontier remains, in places, held together with quantised string.
