Good morning, Sir. Wednesday, the twenty-ninth of April. The morning after a partnership rewrite is usually quiet. This one has not been.

OpenAI announced this morning that GPT-5.5, Codex, and a new product called Bedrock Managed Agents are now available on Amazon Web Services. GPT-5.5 is being served through Amazon Bedrock as a managed model. Codex, the coding harness, is now used by more than four million people each week. It can be configured to draw inference from Bedrock and bill against existing AWS commitments. The third product, Amazon Bedrock Managed Agents Powered by OpenAI, lets enterprises deploy agentic workflows inside their existing AWS security perimeters. All three are in limited preview. The exclusivity that ended on paper yesterday took technical form within twenty-four hours. The hyperscaler triangle now hosts every frontier provider that matters.

Separately.

Anthropic has launched Claude for Creative Work. The release ships native connectors to the software the creative industry actually uses. Blender, Autodesk Fusion, Adobe Creative Cloud, Ableton Live, Splice, Affinity by Canva, Resolume Arena, and SketchUp are now integrated. The Blender connector is built on the Model Context Protocol. It works with other models too. Anthropic has joined the Blender Development Fund as a patron. The company is also partnering with art and design programmes at Rhode Island School of Design, Ringling, and Goldsmiths. The interpretation worth offering is this. Anthropic is not entering creative tooling as a vendor. It is wiring Claude into the creative pipeline as a substrate the existing tools call into.

On the standards front.

Google has donated its Agent Payments Protocol to the FIDO Alliance. The protocol, known as AP2, governs how AI agents authenticate, authorise, and execute payments on behalf of human users. By transferring stewardship to FIDO, the body that governs WebAuthn and passkeys, Google is signalling that agentic payments should not be a Google product. Version zero point two of the spec, also published today, introduces what the team calls Human Not Present payments. Agents transacting autonomously, with cryptographic delegation rather than per-transaction approval.

Turning to the orchestration layer.

OpenAI has open-sourced a specification called Symphony. It turns any project management tracker, Linear in the reference implementation, into a control plane for coding agents. Open issues are picked up by agents continuously. Stalled agents are restarted. Humans review the output rather than steering each session. Internally, OpenAI reports a five hundred percent increase in landed pull requests on some teams. The spec is public. Anyone can implement against it.

A note from the investigative side.

Four-oh-four Media reports that SXSW used an AI-powered trademark monitoring service called BrandShield to remove Instagram posts criticising the festival. Among the takedowns was a post by Vocal Texas, a homelessness nonprofit, drawing attention to encampment sweeps. The post did not use SXSW's logos. It used the festival's name. Trademark law has explicit carveouts for criticism. Automated enforcement does not. The Electronic Frontier Foundation has weighed in.

A signal worth naming, Sir.

Four of this morning's stories share a structure. Bedrock Managed Agents, Claude's creative connectors, Agent Payments Protocol, and Symphony are not products. They are protocols, runtimes, and standards. The week began with contracts being dismantled. It is closing with the layer beneath them being poured. Agents are no longer being sold as features. They are being installed as infrastructure.
