## Full Script

Good morning, Sir. Thursday, the sixteenth of April — and the laboratories have been rather busy overnight.

Google DeepMind has made three announcements today. First, a release post for Gemma 4, their most capable open model family — described as "byte for byte, the most capable open models." The E2B and E4B variants are compact enough to run offline on a phone, while the 26 and 31 billion parameter versions target consumer GPUs for coding and agentic work. Second, Gemini 3.1 Flash TTS — an expressive text-to-speech system representing, in Google's words, the next generation of AI speech synthesis. And third, Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6, focused on enhanced embodied reasoning for real-world physical tasks. A productive morning for DeepMind.

On the Anthropic front — and I realise we return here often, Sir, but today there is genuine substance — Claude Opus 4.7 now appears to be live. Users are reporting its presence on Google Vertex and on Claude.ai directly. Yesterday it was anticipated; today, it would seem, it has arrived quietly.

From Hacker News, a pointed community post titled — bluntly — "Stop Using Ollama." The author argues that Ollama built its reputation on llama.cpp while failing to credit it appropriately, forked it with introduced performance regressions, and has more recently pivoted toward cloud features that quietly undermine its local-first privacy promise. Recommended alternatives include llama.cpp's native server directly, paired with LM Studio or Jan for those who prefer a graphical interface. The piece has generated considerable discussion and more than a few quiet nods among practitioners who have held similar reservations.

A second Hacker News item worth noting: Darkbloom — a new product from Eigen Labs offering decentralised inference on idle Apple Silicon. The premise is that over one hundred million Apple Silicon machines sit underutilised in the world. Darkbloom connects users needing private inference with hardware owners who can earn from idle capacity. Crucially, requests are encrypted before they leave your device. Worth watching, Sir, if private inference economics are of interest.

On the community pulse: practitioners working with the major commercial models are noticing something — a widespread, simultaneous perceived drop in output quality across Claude, GPT, and Gemini. The evidence is anecdotal, but the consistency of the observation is worth flagging. Whether this is model drift, usage pattern changes, or something more systemic remains to be seen.

From 404 Media, a striking piece: Ukraine's President Zelenskyy has claimed that Russian forces surrendered to ground robots — the first documented case of autonomous ground vehicles conducting operations that prompted an enemy surrender without any infantry involvement. Field reporting adds some nuance to the claim, but the underlying trajectory — robots mediating frontline interaction — is difficult to dismiss.

For YouTube: Nate Jones has a new full-length piece out titled "The Real Problem With AI Agents Nobody's Talking About." He also released a short this morning on the Red Queen memo and competitive survival dynamics. The longer piece appears to be the more considered companion to his recent commercial post-mortem, and is worth adding to your queue.
